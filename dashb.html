Business Understanding01

Study Scope

This dashboard analyzes two Pacific Community indicator datasets covering Pacific Island Countries and Territories: annual electricity power generation in gigawatt hours from 2000 to 2023, and annual greenhouse gas emissions per capita in tonnes from 1970 to 2024.

Guiding questions driving the analysis: how have regional power generation and per capita emissions trended over time, which countries are the largest and fastest growing producers and emitters, is there a relationship between a country's power growth and its emissions growth, and can country emission profiles be grouped into meaningful clusters with a usable short term forecast.

Data Mining Sources

Both datasets were exported from the SPC Pacific Data Hub Climate Change Indicators data flow, code SPC underscore DF underscore CLIMATE underscore CHANGE 1.0. Each workbook stores a wide table sheet, country rows by year columns, with a six row metadata header, plus a separate overview sheet. Data were parsed programmatically with Python and pandas directly from the cell grid.

Reading the Trend

Regional power generation rose strongly and near linearly across the full study window, an unusually clean fit for a socioeconomic series at R squared 0.915. The regional GHG per capita average also trended down, R squared 0.666, a looser fit that hints at a compositional story rather than a uniform regional pattern, confirmed later in the cluster analysis.

Methodology and Feature Engineering12
Feature 01

OLS Trend Slope

Per country per indicator, capturing direction and speed of change independent of absolute scale.

Feature 02

Trend R Squared

Captures how linear and predictable the trajectory is versus volatile, used to separate steady movers from noisy ones.

Feature 03

Standardized Series Shape

Z scored time series used as clustering input so Palau's large scale did not simply dominate the clustering by magnitude alone.

Modeling Approach

Two complementary model types were used, appropriate to the scale of this dataset, aggregate annual series rather than high frequency data suited to complex machine learning. Linear regression, OLS, was used for trend estimation and short horizon forecasting of regional totals, appropriate given strong linearity, R squared 0.92 for power, and the risk of overfitting more complex models on roughly 20 to 55 annual points per series. K Means clustering, unsupervised, was used to segment countries by emissions profile for comparative analysis.

Formula, Final Regression Style Read

Total regional power generation follows GWh equals 155.1 multiplied by year plus a constant, R squared 0.915. Average GHG per capita follows tonnes equals negative 0.118 multiplied by year plus a constant, R squared 0.666.

Key Findings13
  • Broad growth in power. Regional generation rose steadily and near linearly from 2000 to 2023, plus 155 GWh per year, R sq 0.92, led by New Caledonia, Papua New Guinea and Fiji.
  • Skewed decline in average emissions. The regional per capita average fell from 1970 to 2024, but this is dominated by Palau easing from a very high base rather than a broad regional cleanup.
  • Weak growth link. Power generation growth and GHG per capita growth show no significant correlation across countries, r equals 0.18, p equals 0.53, meaning fuel mix, bunkering activity and population differences matter more than volume alone.
  • Two structural outliers. Clustering confirms Palau and New Caledonia are distinct from the other fifteen territories and should be reported separately to avoid a skewed regional narrative.
  • Forecast direction. Naive five year linear models project regional power near 11,962 GWh by 2028 and average GHG per capita near 5.77 tonnes by 2029, directional estimates only.
  • Broad based growth, not concentrated. The decade by decade distribution of power values widens and shifts upward across the board, confirming growth is regional rather than a few large countries pulling up the average.
  • Ranking churn beneath the surface. New Caledonia and Fiji climb the producer ranking over time while Guam slips, even though Guam remains a top three producer in absolute terms.
Limitations and Next Steps14

Data Quality Notes

Coverage is uneven, the power series begins in 2000 while the GHG series extends back to 1970, and some small territories, for example Guam and Nauru, show flat, unchanging GHG figures across long stretches, suggesting possible carried forward estimates rather than fresh measurement. This is disclosed rather than silently corrected.

  • No population join. Total, not just per capita, emissions trends could not be computed, a valuable next step.
  • No fuel mix breakdown. Renewable versus fossil detail was not available in this extract, and it would materially improve the power to emissions linkage analysis.
  • Annual granularity only. Longer, more granular, for example monthly, data would support more rigorous time series forecasting such as ARIMA instead of linear extrapolation.
  • Flat series need verification. Unchanging series for some small territories should be checked against SPC metadata before being used for policy conclusions.
